# Provenzano's Hat Trick Fuels Braves Comeback, but Sugo Sundays Prevails 6–4

*Four-point performance and a late power-play equalizer aren't enough as the Braves suffer another narrow finish.*

**VAUGHAN, Ontario —** Michael Provenzano scored three times, including a third-period power-play equalizer, but Brave H.C. surrendered two late goals in a 6–4 loss to Sugo Sundays on Sunday night at Vaughan Sports Village's A Rink.

Provenzano's four-point performance brought the Braves back from a 4–2 deficit and tied the game with 4:36 remaining. Sugo reclaimed the lead just 46 seconds later, and a subsequent empty-netter ended Brave H.C.'s hopes of completing the comeback.

It was a familiar finish. A week earlier, the Braves had erased a three-goal deficit against Other Way before losing 6–5 on a goal with just 10 seconds remaining.

## Pregame — A Changing Lineup

The Braves entered at 1–6, having scored 13 goals over their previous three games. Sugo Sundays came in at 3–3–1, with 27 goals scored and 34 allowed through seven contests.

Full-time players Alex Pagnotta, Keith Pearsoll, Mario Provenzano and Anthony Paolucci did not dress. Call-ups Krishnan Rajasooriar and Damien Pavaday joined returning call-up Aaron Carosi, who made his first recorded Braves appearance since February 25, 2018—almost nine years ago—and dressed alongside his brother Justin.

Pearsoll, meanwhile, coached his son's hometown Vaughan Rangers U18 Select team to a championship at its tournament at Vaughan Sports Village's A Rink. He still managed to stop by late in the Braves' first period to check in on his teammates.

## First Period — Saindon Opens the Scoring

Stephane Saindon gave the Braves a 1–0 lead at 9:47 of the first, converting a play set up by Provenzano and Francesco Triumbari. The goal continued Saindon's recent production after his two-goal, three-point performance against Other Way.

Sugo answered at 7:42 to make it 1–1. The visitors took a body-contact penalty at 6:10, but Brave H.C. could not capitalize on the power play, and the teams entered the intermission tied.

## Second Period — Provenzano Answers

Sugo moved ahead 2–1 at 6:38 of the second period, but Provenzano tied it at 3:38 on a goal assisted by Justin Carosi.

The deadlock lasted only until Sugo's next power play. Adam Povegliano was penalized for tripping at 2:43, and Sugo converted at 1:31 to take a 3–2 lead into the third.

## Third Period — Hat Trick, Comeback and a Costly Finish

Sugo extended its advantage to 4–2 at 9:19 of the third, forcing the Braves to chase another multi-goal deficit.

Provenzano responded at 5:43, scoring his second of the night with assists from Povegliano and Saindon to pull Brave H.C. within one. Sugo was penalized for tripping at 5:12, giving the Braves another chance with the man advantage.

At 4:36, Provenzano completed his hat trick on the power play, with Justin Carosi and captain Adam Brutto earning assists. The goal tied the game 4–4 and capped a two-goal burst in just 67 seconds.

But Sugo answered at 3:50 to regain the lead, 5–4. With the Braves trailing and time running out, Brutto pulled goaltender Stephen Logue for an extra attacker. At 3:15, Sugo scored an empty-netter to seal the 6–4 result.

"Yeah, probably should have waited a bit," Brutto said afterward. "I guess the guys didn't realize Steve was pulled. I gotta communicate that better with the assistant captains next time."

The captain said he believed the Braves still had a chance to force another equalizer.

"I just felt like we had a chance maybe to tie it up there, you know. Just didn't go as planned this time, but we'll learn from it and try and turn this season around like we did last year."

## Provenzano's Four-Point Night

Provenzano earned first Player of the Game honours with three goals and an assist. His goals tied the contest in the second, narrowed the deficit in the third and completed the comeback on the power play.

Saindon was named second Player of the Game after recording a goal and an assist, giving him five points over his last two appearances. Justin Carosi collected two assists, while Povegliano, Brutto and Triumbari each added one.

Stephen Logue earned third Player of the Game honours. He was charged with five goals against, with Sugo's sixth coming into an empty net. The Braves finished with four goals and seven assists.

## The Bigger Picture

The loss dropped Brave H.C. to 1–7, with 23 goals scored and 42 allowed through eight games.

After scoring just six goals over their first four contests, the Braves have produced 17 in their last four—4.25 per game. But they have surrendered 23 over that same stretch, averaging 5.75 against.

The offensive improvement is real, and the ability to erase deficits has become a recurring theme. So has the difficulty closing games. Against Other Way and Sugo, the Braves staged substantial comebacks without earning a point in the standings.

Brutto's postgame comments captured both the frustration and the intention to learn from another late setback. Provenzano's hat trick offered plenty of evidence that the Braves can compete offensively. Their next challenge is converting those performances into results.

## Next Up — Dateline Predators

The Braves return to Vaughan Sports Village on Sunday, November 22, to face Dateline Predators, who beat them 3–0 in their September 20 season opener.

Brave H.C. has scored 17 goals in its last four games, a sharp contrast to that opening-night shutout. The rematch offers an opportunity to measure the progress—and, more importantly, to turn it into a win.
