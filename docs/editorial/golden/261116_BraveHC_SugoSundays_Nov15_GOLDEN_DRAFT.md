# Provenzano's Hat Trick Fuels Braves Comeback, but Sugo Sundays Prevails 6–4

*Four-point performance, Aaron Carosi's return after almost nine years and a hometown U18 championship highlight an eventful Sunday at A Rink.*

**VAUGHAN, Ontario —** Michael Provenzano scored three goals, including a dramatic third-period power-play equalizer, but Brave H.C. surrendered two late goals in a 6–4 loss to Sugo Sundays on Sunday night at Vaughan Sports Village's A Rink.

Provenzano's four-point performance helped the Braves erase a 4–2 deficit, completing his hat trick with 4:36 remaining to tie the contest. Sugo reclaimed the lead 46 seconds later, before adding an empty-netter.

For the second consecutive week, Brave H.C. battled back from a multi-goal deficit only to suffer defeat. Seven days earlier, the Braves erased a 3–0 deficit against Other Way before falling 6–5 on a goal with just 10 seconds remaining.

Sunday also featured two personal storylines: Aaron Carosi's return after almost nine years and Keith Pearsoll's championship weekend coaching his son's hometown Vaughan Rangers U18 Select team.

## Pregame — Familiar Faces and a Championship at A Rink

The Braves entered at 1–6, having scored 13 goals in their previous three games. Sugo Sundays arrived at 3–3–1, with 27 goals scored and 34 allowed through seven contests.

Full-time players Alex Pagnotta, Keith Pearsoll, Mario Provenzano and Anthony Paolucci did not dress. Call-ups Krishnan Rajasooriar and Damien Pavaday joined the lineup, along with returning call-up defenceman Aaron Carosi.

**The Carosi brothers reunite after almost nine years.** Aaron Carosi, No. 59, made his first recorded Braves appearance since February 25, 2018, when he played in a 5–3 loss to Hockey Team. The defenceman, who first appeared with the club in 2015, reunited on the ice with brother Justin, who would contribute two assists in Sunday's game.

**Keith Pearsoll's championship Sunday.** Pearsoll was coaching his son's hometown Vaughan Rangers U18 Select team, which, according to the captain's account, won its division at the Vaughan Rangers Select Memorial Tournament. The tournament was taking place at Vaughan Sports Village's A Rink, the same rink where the Braves faced Sugo. Despite his coaching commitments, Pearsoll stopped by during the latter stages of the first period to check in on his Braves teammates.

## First Period — Saindon Strikes First

After falling behind 3–0 in the first period against Other Way, the Braves made a stronger start. At 9:47, Stephane Saindon opened the scoring, assisted by Provenzano and Francesco Triumbari, to give Brave H.C. a 1–0 lead.

The goal continued Saindon's productive stretch following his two-goal, three-point performance against Other Way. Sugo answered at 7:42 to tie it 1–1. The visitors took a two-minute body-contact penalty at 6:10, but the Braves could not convert. The teams entered the intermission level.

## Second Period — Provenzano Answers, but Sugo's Power Play Delivers

Sugo took its first lead at 6:38 of the second period. Provenzano responded at 3:38, scoring off an assist from Justin Carosi to tie the game 2–2.

At 2:43, Adam Povegliano received a two-minute tripping penalty. Sugo capitalized at 1:31 with a power-play goal to reclaim a 3–2 lead heading into the third.

## Third Period — A Hat Trick and a Costly Extra-Attacker Gamble

Sugo extended its advantage to 4–2 at 9:19, leaving Brave H.C. facing another two-goal deficit.

Provenzano sparked the comeback at 5:43, scoring his second of the night with assists from Povegliano and Saindon to cut the deficit to 4–3. Just 31 seconds later, Sugo was penalized for tripping.

At 4:36, Provenzano completed his hat trick with a power-play goal, assisted by Justin Carosi and captain Adam Brutto. The goal tied the contest 4–4 and gave Provenzano his fourth point of the night. Carosi had his second assist; Brutto collected a point on the equalizer.

The Braves had scored twice in 67 seconds, but the comeback would not last. At 3:50, Sugo scored to go ahead 5–4. With his team trailing and time running out, Brutto decided to pull goaltender Stephen Logue for an extra attacker. At 3:15, Sugo scored an empty-netter to secure the 6–4 victory.

"Yeah, probably should have waited a bit," Brutto said afterward. "I guess the guys didn't realize Steve was pulled. I gotta communicate that better with the assistant captains next time."

Brutto said he believed there was still an opportunity to draw level.

"I just felt like we had a chance maybe to tie it up there, you know. Just didn't go as planned this time, but we'll learn from it and try and turn this season around like we did last year."

For the second consecutive Sunday, Brave H.C. had erased a multi-goal deficit only to see the game slip away late.

## Provenzano Leads the Way

Provenzano earned first Player of the Game honours with three goals and an assist for four points. His first goal tied the game in the second period, his second brought the Braves within one in the third, and his third completed the hat trick on the power play.

Saindon earned second Player of the Game recognition with a goal and an assist, giving him five points over his last two appearances. Justin Carosi contributed two assists on the night of his brother Aaron's long-awaited return. Povegliano, Brutto and Triumbari each added an assist, ensuring six Braves skaters recorded at least one point.

Stephen Logue earned third Player of the Game honours. Five goals were charged against him; the final goal was scored into an empty net according to the captain's account, pending correction of the official game sheet.

The Braves finished with four goals, seven assists and 11 scoring points.

## The Bigger Picture — More Goals, but Results Remain Elusive

The defeat dropped Brave H.C. to 1–7 through eight games, with 23 goals scored and 42 conceded.

After scoring six goals in their first four contests, the Braves have produced 17 over their last four, averaging 4.25 per game. But they have also allowed 23 in that span, an average of 5.75.

Against Other Way, they recovered from three goals down before losing in the final seconds. Against Sugo, they erased a two-goal third-period deficit before surrendering two late goals.

Provenzano's hat trick demonstrated the team's improving offence, while Aaron Carosi's return and Pearsoll's U18 tournament championship supplied memorable moments beyond the result. Brutto's candid assessment of the late goalie pull offered another lesson for a team determined to turn its season around.

## Next Up — A Rematch with Dateline Predators

Brave H.C. returns to Vaughan Sports Village on Sunday, November 22, to face Dateline Predators, who shut out the Braves 3–0 in their September 20 season opener.

The Braves have scored 17 goals over their last four contests, recorded a hat trick and produced consecutive multi-goal comebacks. Yet their record remains 1–7.

The challenge is no longer simply finding ways to score. It is finding ways to make those goals count when the final whistle sounds.
