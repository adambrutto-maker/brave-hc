# Provenzano's Hat Trick Sparks Braves Comeback, but Sugo Sundays Wins 6–4

*Four-point night and late power-play equalizer bring Brave H.C. level before two Sugo goals decide another dramatic finish.*

**VAUGHAN, Ontario —** Michael Provenzano scored a hat trick, including a third-period power-play equalizer, but Brave H.C. couldn't complete the comeback in a 6–4 loss to Sugo Sundays on Sunday night at Vaughan Sports Village's A Rink.

Trailing 4–2 in the third, the Braves scored twice in 67 seconds to tie the game with 4:36 remaining. Sugo answered 46 seconds later, and an empty-net goal sealed the result.

It was the second straight week the Braves had erased a multi-goal deficit without earning a point. Against Other Way a week earlier, they fought back from 3–0 before falling 6–5 on a goal with 10 seconds left.

## Pregame — Familiar Faces, Changing Lineup

Brave H.C. entered at 1–6 after scoring 13 goals over its previous three games. Sugo arrived at 3–3–1, with 27 goals scored and 34 allowed in seven contests.

Full-time players Alex Pagnotta, Keith Pearsoll, Mario Provenzano and Anthony Paolucci did not dress. Call-ups Krishnan Rajasooriar and Damien Pavaday were joined by Aaron Carosi, making his first recorded Braves appearance since February 25, 2018—almost nine years ago—and dressing alongside brother Justin.

Pearsoll was coaching his son's hometown Vaughan Rangers U18 Select team to a tournament championship at the same A Rink. He stopped by late in the Braves' first period to check in with his teammates.

## First Period — Saindon Opens the Scoring

Stephane Saindon struck at 9:47, finishing a play assisted by Provenzano and Francesco Triumbari to put the Braves ahead 1–0. It continued his productive stretch after a two-goal, three-point performance against Other Way.

Sugo tied it at 7:42. The visitors took a body-contact penalty at 6:10, but the Braves couldn't convert, and the teams finished the period level at 1–1.

## Second Period — Answers at Both Ends

Sugo moved ahead 2–1 at 6:38. Provenzano responded at 3:38, scoring his first goal of the night from Justin Carosi to tie the game.

Adam Povegliano was assessed a tripping penalty at 2:43, and Sugo converted on the power play at 1:31 to take a 3–2 lead into the third.

## Third Period — Hat Trick, Goalie Pull and a Costly Finish

Sugo stretched its lead to 4–2 at 9:19, but Provenzano brought the Braves back into contention. At 5:43, he scored his second goal, assisted by Povegliano and Saindon, to cut the deficit to one.

Sugo was penalized for tripping at 5:12. The Braves capitalized at 4:36 when Provenzano completed his hat trick on the power play, with Justin Carosi and captain Adam Brutto drawing assists. Two goals in 67 seconds had turned a two-goal deficit into a 4–4 tie.

The momentum shifted again almost immediately. Sugo scored at 3:50 to regain a 5–4 lead. Brutto then pulled goaltender Stephen Logue for an extra attacker, hoping the Braves could produce one more equalizer. Instead, Sugo scored into the empty net at 3:15 to make it 6–4.

Brutto said the decision reflected the confidence he had in a suddenly productive offence, especially with Provenzano in form.

"Mikey was hot," the captain said. With the Braves generating more goals in recent weeks, he believed they still had a chance to tie the game.

Brutto also took responsibility for the timing and communication: "Yeah, probably should have waited a bit." He acknowledged the players may not have realized Logue had been pulled and said he needed to communicate more clearly with the alternate captains.

## A Standout Night — and a Defensive Challenge

Provenzano earned first Player of the Game honours with three goals and an assist. Saindon was second with a goal and an assist, giving him five points in his last two appearances. Justin Carosi had two assists; Povegliano, Brutto and Triumbari added one each.

Logue earned third Player of the Game honours. He was charged with five goals against, while Sugo's sixth came into an empty net.

"Steve made some huge stops for us. We just hung him out to dry a bit," Brutto said.

Looking ahead, the captain wants the Braves to strengthen their 1-2-2 structure when forechecking and defending through neutral ice, then transition to tighter man-on-man coverage in the defensive zone. Better defensive assignments and moving recovered pucks toward the boards and out to neutral territory are priorities—not changes the team claims to have mastered already.

## The Bigger Picture — Scoring More, Still Conceding Too Much

The loss dropped Brave H.C. to 1–7, with 23 goals scored and 42 allowed through eight games.

After scoring just six times in its first four games, the team has produced 17 goals over the last four—4.25 per game. It has also allowed 23 in that stretch, an average of 5.75.

The pattern is increasingly clear: Brave H.C. can fight back, but has struggled to close out those recoveries. Provenzano's hat trick showed how dangerous the attack can be. The next step is supporting it with better coverage and cleaner exits.

Brutto remains focused on the season ahead. "We'll learn from it and try and turn this season around like we did last year," he said.

## Next Up — Dateline Predators

The Braves return to Vaughan Sports Village on Sunday, November 22, to face Dateline Predators, who won their September 20 season opener 3–0.

The rematch will test whether Brave H.C.'s recent offensive improvement can be paired with the defensive discipline needed to turn a competitive performance into a victory.
